# A checklist before using it at work

<p class="ep-meta"><span>Ch. 8 · 8.1</span><span>About 6 min read</span><span>2026-11-09</span></p>

!!! abstract "What you'll learn on this page"
    - AI helps a lot with some jobs. With others, ==it can actually make you less likely to get the right answer==.
    - Before you bring AI into your work, check the kind of job, whether you can check it, the risk, and how sensitive it is, with five questions.
    - Try it small, and measure before and after to decide.

## A road that isn't on the map

A bike is fast on a flat road. In front of a staircase, it just gets in the way. You need to know which paths you can ride on.

A large experiment in 2023 showed this. 758 consultants at the Boston Consulting Group worked on 18 realistic work tasks, some with AI (GPT-4) and some without. On tasks **inside what AI could do**, the AI users finished 12.2% more tasks on average, worked 25.1% faster, and turned in work rated more than 40% higher in quality than a control group. But the researchers also picked a complex business task that they expected to be **outside what AI could do**. On that task, the AI users were 19 percentage points less likely to get the right answer. The researchers called this the **jagged technological frontier**. Two jobs that look about equally hard can fall on opposite sides: AI helps with one and hurts the other.

!!! tip "In 10-year-old words"
    On some paths AI is a bike. On others, it's a bike in front of a staircase. Find out which path you're on first.

## Five questions

Before you bring AI into a job, ask these five questions.

| Question | What to check | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Is it inside the frontier? | Has AI handled similar jobs? Have you tried it small? | A complex job you've never tried with AI |
| 2. Can you check the result? | Would you notice if it were wrong? | Using results in a field you don't know |
| 3. How bad is a mistake? | Does it touch money, health, law, or reputation? | One mistake costs a lot and is hard to undo |
| 4. Is sensitive information involved? | Personal details or secrets? (Chapter 3) | Information you can't blank out |
| 5. Does it follow the rules? | Your workplace's, school's, or the law's? | You don't know the rules |

**Scoring:** If there are no warning signs, go ahead. If there's even one, first decide how to handle it: add a checking step, blank things out, or get approval. If question 3 is serious, use AI results only as a reference.

## Try it small

1. **Pick just one job.** Start with the one that repeats most and has the least risk.
2. **Measure "before."** Write down how long the job takes without AI, and how good the result is. If you have no records, measure it once this week.
3. **Use AI for two weeks.** Do the same job with AI, and write down the time, number of mistakes, and how happy you are with it.
4. **Compare.** If it's faster but has more mistakes, that's not a win.

Anthropic's guide to testing says to set success criteria that are **specific and measurable**, and to check them with test cases. The same goes for your own work. Compare with numbers, not with "it feels better."

```text
[Trial log]
Job: ___
Before: ___ minutes, ___ mistakes
After 2 weeks: ___ minutes, ___ mistakes
How I checked: ___
Keep using it? Yes / No / With conditions (conditions: ___)
```

!!! note "★ Going deeper"
    - In the study, the "outside" task was one the researchers chose on purpose to be hard for AI. So it doesn't mean "AI usually gets things wrong." The key point is that **the edge is invisible and jagged**.
    - The study used GPT-4 in 2023. Models and the ways people use them have changed a lot since then. You can't assume the same numbers hold today. A habit of testing each kind of job yourself lasts longer.
    - One problem the study points to: people may lean too hard on AI and accept wrong answers as is. That's why it matters to build checking into the job itself.

## Try it yourself

Pick one job you want to bring AI into, and fill in the five-question table above.

1. Which questions show a warning sign?
2. How will you handle each one? (A checking step, blanking things out, getting approval, and so on.)
3. Make a two-week trial log and fill in the first line.

## Use it today

1. **Decide the risk first.** Question 3 is where every decision starts.
2. **Measure "before."** Without a record, you can't tell what AI changed.
3. **Build a checking step into the job.** Don't hand AI a job whose results you can't check.
4. **Grow from small jobs.** Start where it works, and widen one step at a time.
5. **Review every couple of months.** As models and services change, the edge moves too.

## Common mix-ups

- "AI always makes things faster." Depending on the job, it can make you faster, or make you more likely to be wrong.
- "The harder the job, the more AI helps." In the study, a complex task outside AI's range got worse with AI.
- "Testing takes too long, so I'll skip it." Measuring just one job for two weeks can show a big difference.
