# Rules your team agrees on

<p class="ep-meta"><span>Ch. 8 · 8.1</span><span>About 5 min read</span><span>2026-11-09</span></p>

!!! abstract "What you'll learn on this page"
    - For a team to use AI together, agree ahead of time on ==what can go in, who checks the results, and how to say AI was used==.
    - Rules should be short and easy to follow. One page is enough.
    - When something goes wrong, focus on fixing it, not on blaming someone.

## Classroom rules

A classroom with no rules has arguments. A classroom with too many rules has rules nobody follows. Short, clear rules like "Walk in the hallway" are the ones people keep.

Teams using AI are the same. If everyone does it their own way, someone may paste in customer details, and someone else may hide that they used AI. Later, that turns into trouble.

!!! tip "In 10-year-old words"
    Make the rules short and clear, and fit them on one page.

## What goes on a one-page rule sheet

| Section | What to decide | Example |
|---|---|---|
| OK uses | What AI may be used for | Drafts, summaries, ideas, sorting tables |
| Not OK uses | What it may not be used for | Entering customer personal details, final legal judgments |
| Data levels | What may be typed in | Public: OK. Internal: approved tools only. Sensitive: never |
| Who checks | Who reviews the results | The writer checks first; the manager checks anything sent outside |
| Saying so | How to note AI use | Outside documents say "AI helped draft this" |
| Approved tools | Which tools to use | [The tool on your company account] |
| If something goes wrong | Who to tell | A contact name and how to reach them |

## Sorting data into levels

This is the most important part of the rules. It turns the privacy ideas from Chapter 3 into your team's own words.

- **Public:** Material that's already public. Usually OK to type in, but check for personal details and copyright or sharing limits.
- **Internal:** Company documents and plans that aren't public yet. Only in tools your workplace has approved.
- **Sensitive:** Customer personal details, contracts, HR records, health information. Don't type it in, or only with details blanked out.

## Making the rules stick

1. **Keep it to one page.** If it's long, people won't read it.
2. **Add examples.** "This is fine, this isn't" gets understood faster than rules alone.
3. **Decide together.** Rules are more realistic when the people doing the work help make them.
4. **Say who to ask.** When something's unclear, people need someone to ask.
5. **Update every three months.** Services and laws change.
6. **Make it easy to report mistakes.** Hidden mistakes grow into bigger problems.

!!! note "★ Going deeper"
    - On January 26, 2023, the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) released its **AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)**. It helps organizations that build and use AI make it more trustworthy, and it's meant for voluntary use. On July 26, 2024, NIST added a Generative AI Profile (NIST-AI-600-1) that covers risks special to generative AI. If you want to build team rules in a more formal way, these are good references. For individuals and small teams, a one-page sheet like this one is more realistic.
    - This page is general information, not legal advice. Set your company's official policy together with your legal and security teams.

## Try it yourself

Draft a one-page rule sheet for your team (or club).

1. Fill in the seven sections from the table above.
2. Ask two teammates to read it and tell you, "What's unclear?"
3. Fix the unclear parts and share it with the team.

## Use it today

1. **Start with the three data levels.** That alone prevents a lot of serious trouble.
2. **Name one person to ask when unsure.** One name is enough.
3. **Have a person check anything that goes outside the team.** Even if AI wrote it, the sender is responsible.
4. **Decide how to say AI was used.** Hiding it is the riskiest choice.
5. **Show examples when you share the rules.** They make the rules click faster.

## Common mix-ups

- "Rules make people use AI less." Knowing the safe zone actually lets people use it with confidence.
- "Banning it keeps us safe." If you only ban it, some people may use it secretly. Show them a safe way instead.
- "Once we set the rules, we're done." Services and laws change, so check every three months.
