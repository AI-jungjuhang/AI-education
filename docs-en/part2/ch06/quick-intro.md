# Get to know a new field fast

<p class="ep-meta"><span>Ch. 6 · 6.1</span><span>About 5 min read</span><span>2026-11-09</span></p>

!!! abstract "What you'll learn on this page"
    - To get the hang of a new field fast, start with ==a map of the big picture and a list of key words==.
    - AI's beginner explanations are simplified and can be wrong. Use them as a map, and find evidence elsewhere.
    - Finish with a list of questions to ask next, so you keep learning on your own.

## A map of a new city

When you visit a new city, you look at a map first. Once you roughly know where things are, you won't get lost in the side streets.

A new field is the same. If you jump straight into expert material, it's full of words you don't know, and you get tired fast. AI is good at quickly drawing a map of the whole field and a list of key terms. But the map isn't always right, so check it as you go.

!!! tip "In 10-year-old words"
    Ask AI to draw you a map of the new city. Then walk around and check that the map is right.

## Five steps to get the lay of the land

**Step 1: One easy paragraph.** Ask for an explanation "in one paragraph, as if to a grade schooler." That gives you the big picture.

**Step 2: Ten key words.** Get the 10 terms that come up most in this field, each with a simple explanation.

**Step 3: A map.** Ask for the smaller areas inside the field and how they connect, laid out so you can see them at a glance.

**Step 4: Mix-ups and debates.** Ask what beginners often get wrong, and where experts disagree.

**Step 5: Where to go next.** Ask what kinds of material to read to go deeper, and which questions to ask.

```text
I'm a [job / grade] and I'm brand new to [field]. Tell me, in this order:
1) A one-paragraph explanation a grade schooler could follow
2) The 10 most common terms, with a one-line explanation each
3) A map of the smaller areas in this field (a table or a list)
4) 3 common beginner mix-ups, and the questions experts disagree on
5) 5 questions to ask as I learn more
Mark anything you're not sure about as "not sure."
```

## Trust the map only so far

- **Check the terms one by one.** Look up each new term once in a trustworthy source.
- **Search for book and course titles yourself.** Use the checks from Chapter 3 to see if the books or courses it names really exist.
- **Pay attention to the "debates" part.** Where experts disagree, AI tends to give only one side, or to oversimplify.
- **Beginner explanations are simplified.** Later, when you read expert material, you'll think, "Ah, the first explanation was too simple," and fix your picture.

!!! note "★ Going deeper"
    - AI knows well-known fields with lots of material fairly well. It gets more wrong in brand-new or very narrow fields (remember hallucination from Chapter 1). Ask yourself: "If I already knew this field, could I spot what's wrong?" The less you know, the harder it is to notice its mistakes.
    - So it's safer to pick one beginner book by an expert, or one official guide, as your **anchor**. Then compare what AI tells you with that anchor.

## Try it yourself

Pick a field you're curious about and use the template above. Then, with the answer:

1. Pick 3 of the 10 terms, look them up, and see if the explanations hold up.
2. If it named any books or materials, check that they really exist.
3. Pick the most interesting question and look into it yourself.

## Use it today

1. **Draw the map in a day.** If you have a meeting about an unfamiliar topic, use this template the day before to get the big picture.
2. **Save the term list.** You can pull it up when you get stuck reading.
3. **Ask it to mark what it's "not sure" about.** That shows you where to check.
4. **Choose one anchor source.** A beginner book, an official guide, or a trusted course.
5. **Make a list of questions for an expert.** The questions from your map help a lot.

## Common mix-ups

- "When AI explains it, that's the same as an expert explaining it." Beginner explanations are simplified.
- "If I read it once, I understand it." You only know you understand when you can explain it or solve a problem. Later pages cover this.
- "I don't know this field, so I have no choice but to trust AI." The less you know, the more you need an anchor to check against.
