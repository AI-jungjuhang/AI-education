# Use it as a study buddy

<p class="ep-meta"><span>Ch. 6 · 6.1</span><span>About 6 min read</span><span>2026-11-09</span></p>

!!! abstract "What you'll learn on this page"
    - For studying, use AI as ==a practice partner that quizzes you==, not as an answer machine. What you learn sticks better.
    - Research shows that trying to recall what you read sticks longer than reading it again.
    - Use three methods: ask for an explanation, get quizzed, and have your own explanation checked.

## Copying from the answer key

If you just copy from the answer key in a math workbook, it feels like you solved the problems. But on the test, you can't. Your brain does its work while you try to solve them.

If you keep asking AI "explain this," you get the feeling that you understand. But **feeling that you understand is not the same as remembering**.

!!! tip "In 10-year-old words"
    Don't just copy down AI's answers. Have AI give you questions, and you answer them.

## Recalling beats rereading

In a 2006 study in the journal *Psychological Science*, Henry Roediger and Jeffrey Karpicke had students read a passage. Then some took a test where they tried to recall it, and others simply read it again. On a test five minutes later, the rereaders did better. But on tests two days and one week later, **the students who had taken the recall test remembered much more**. The rereaders also felt more sure of their memory, even though they did worse later. The researchers say tests aren't only for measuring what you know. They're a powerful way to learn.

So "quiz me" is closer to lasting learning than asking AI to "explain it again" over and over.

## Three ways to study

**1. Get an explanation, then a quiz right away**

```text
Explain [topic] so a 6th grader could understand it.
When you're done, ask me 3 questions to check that I understood.
Don't give me the answers until I've answered.
```

**2. Get quizzed**

```text
Give me 5 questions on [what I just studied], from easy to hard.
Ask one at a time. When I answer, tell me if I'm right.
If I'm wrong, give me a hint first.
```

**3. Have your explanation checked**

```text
Here's how I understand [topic]. Tell me what's wrong or missing.
<my explanation>
(write it the way you understand it)
</my explanation>
```

The third one is especially good. **Explaining something in your own words** is studying all by itself.

## Limits of a study buddy

- **AI's explanations can be wrong.** If they differ from your textbook or your class, the textbook wins.
- **Its grading can be wrong too.** For questions with set answers, check the answer key.
- **Watch out for the habit of getting answers right away.** When you get one wrong, ask for a hint first. That's what makes your brain work.

!!! note "★ Going deeper"
    - In April 2025, Anthropic announced Claude for Education for universities, along with a **learning mode**. Instead of handing over answers, it guides the student's reasoning. The idea is that a helper that makes you think is better for learning than one that gives answers. Check whether the service you use has something similar.
    - The study tested reading a passage and recalling it. Whether the same works for other kinds of studying, like solving math or writing code, needs its own look.
    - The study also shows that for a test right away, like five minutes later, rereading can work better. Pick your method based on whether you want to cram for now or remember for a long time.

## Try it yourself

Pick one thing you studied this week.

1. Use method 1 to get an explanation, and answer the 3 questions.
2. A day later, solve 5 questions on the same topic with method 2.
3. A week later, without AI, write down everything you remember on paper. Then use method 3 to have it checked.

How much do you remember after a week? Compare it with a topic you only reread.

## Use it today

1. **After "explain this," add "now quiz me."** One line is all it takes.
2. **Ask it to hold the answer until you've answered.** That's what turns it into recall practice.
3. **Space it out.** Studying once today, again in two days, and again in a week is known to work better than all at once. (This spacing effect is a widely known learning principle, not a finding from the study above.)
4. **Explain it in your own words and get it checked.** It's the easiest method of all.
5. **Compare AI's explanation with your textbook.** For tests, your textbook and teacher set the standard.

## Common mix-ups

- "If AI explains it simply, I've learned it." Feeling that you understand is different from remembering.
- "Rereading makes me feel confident, so it works." In the study, rereaders felt more confident, but a week later the recall group remembered more.
- "If AI does my homework, it does my studying for me." Then nothing stays in your head.
