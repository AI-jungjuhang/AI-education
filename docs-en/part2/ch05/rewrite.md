# Rewrite and change the tone

<p class="ep-meta"><span>Ch. 5 · 5.1</span><span>About 5 min read</span><span>2026-11-09</span></p>

!!! abstract "What you'll learn on this page"
    - One of the jobs AI helps with most reliably is ==polishing something you already wrote==.
    - Always say: "keep the meaning the same, and don't add facts."
    - Ask it to show what it changed, so checking is easy.

## A change of clothes

The same person wears different clothes to a job interview and to a picnic. In the same way, the same message needs different clothes for different readers. An apology to a client should be polite. A text to a friend can be casual.

Rewriting is safer than drafting, because the content already comes from you. That leaves less room for the hallucinations from Chapter 1. Still, while polishing, it can quietly change or add something.

!!! tip "In 10-year-old words"
    Keep the message the same and just change its clothes. The body inside shouldn't change while you're changing the outfit.

## Kinds of rewriting

| What you want | What to say |
|---|---|
| Spelling and grammar only | "Fix only spelling and grammar. Don't change the sentences." |
| Easier to read | "Break up long sentences and use simpler words. Keep the meaning." |
| A different tone | "Make it polite and professional, with the same meaning." |
| Shorter | "Cut it in half without losing the meaning." |
| A different mood | "Make it warmer, not cold. Keep the facts the same." |

## Two safety lines to always add

When you ask for a rewrite, make a habit of adding these two lines.

```text
Don't change the meaning, and don't add any facts or numbers that aren't
in the original. List what you changed in the form [original → new].
```

With a list of changes, you can see at a glance what's different. That makes sneaky changes easy to catch.

## Keep your own voice

If you want a certain tone, a sample works faster than a description (Chapter 2).

```text
Rewrite the "original" in the style of the "sample." Follow the sample's
sentence length, word choice, and mood. Don't change the content.
<sample>(something I wrote)</sample>
<original>(the text to fix)</original>
```

!!! note "★ Going deeper"
    - Why does AI sometimes shift the meaning while rewriting? Because rewriting is still "picking the next word." It looks at your sentences and picks new ones that mean about the same thing. Small differences can creep in. Numbers, negatives ("did not"), and words of degree ("almost," "always") are the easiest to change by accident.
    - So put the rewrite next to the original and check those three first: **numbers, negatives, and words of degree**.

## Try it yourself

Pick something you wrote lately, like one paragraph of an email or report.

1. Ask it to make the text easier to read, and add the two safety lines.
2. Use the list of changes to compare with the original.
3. Check whether anything that shouldn't change did change, like numbers, promises, or negatives.

Did anything change? If so, what kind of thing was it?

## Use it today

1. **Add "keep the meaning, don't add facts" by default.** That one line prevents a lot of trouble.
2. **Get a list of changes.** Knowing what changed makes checking easy.
3. **Check numbers, negatives, and words of degree on their own.** They can flip the meaning.
4. **Get versions for different readers.** Ask for "a version for my boss" and "a version for a friend" at once, and compare.
5. **For writing that matters, read the final version yourself.** Once you send it, it's yours.

## Common mix-ups

- "Rewriting is safe, so I don't need to check." It's on the safer side, but numbers or negatives can still change.
- "If I describe the tone, it'll get it right." A sample is more precise.
- "AI is perfect at spelling." It usually does well, but it can get names or technical terms wrong.
