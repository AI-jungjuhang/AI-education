# Personal and sensitive information

<p class="ep-meta"><span>Ch. 3 · 3.2</span><span>About 5 min read</span><span>2026-11-09</span></p>

!!! abstract "What you'll learn on this page"
    - Typing into an online chatbot means ==sending what you type to the company that runs it==. (A model that runs only on your own computer is different.)
    - Blank out personal details, like names, phone numbers, and account numbers, and keep work secrets out.
    - How your chats are used depends on the service and your settings, so you have to check.

!!! warning "Please note"
    This page is general information, not legal advice. Your workplace or school may have its own rules, and those come first.

## Writing on a postcard

Anyone who handles a postcard can read it, mail carriers included. To keep a message private, you put it in an envelope.

Typing into a chatbot is a bit like writing a postcard. If it's an online service, your words don't stay on your computer. They travel to the company's servers. (A model that runs only on your own computer is different.) How the company stores and uses them depends on the service and your settings.

!!! tip "In 10-year-old words"
    Typing into a chatbot is a lot like writing on a postcard. Before you write, cover up anything that would be a problem if someone else saw it.

## What to blank out

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Things that identify you | Name, phone number, home address, Social Security number, bank account number, photos of your face |
| Sensitive details | Health conditions, medical history, religion, political views, sex life |
| Other people's details | Names and private situations of friends, customers, or coworkers |
| Work information | Plans that aren't public yet, customer lists, code, contracts |
| Login details | Passwords, verification codes, API keys |

## How to blank things out

1. **Change the names.** "Jordan Smith" becomes "Person A." "123 Oak Street, Springfield" becomes "a house in a small town."
2. **Hide the numbers.** Delete account numbers and ID numbers completely. Keep amounts only if the question needs them.
3. **Paste only what you need.** Don't paste a whole document. Pick the paragraph your question is about.
4. **Ask about the general situation.** Instead of "our customer Jordan at Acme Corp," try "a customer who complained about this."

```text
In the text below, find personal details such as names, phone numbers,
addresses, and account numbers. Rewrite the text with them replaced by
[Name1], [Phone1], and so on. Don't list what you replaced.
```

(Be careful: if you ask a chatbot to do the blanking, you've already sent it the original. When you can, blank things out yourself.)

## Check how your chats are used

How a service handles what you type depends on the company, your plan, and your settings. Look in your app's **settings and privacy policy** for answers to these questions:

- Are my chats used to improve the model? Can I turn that off?
- How long are my chats kept?
- What happens when I delete a chat?
- If I use a work account, can my employer see my chats?

!!! note "★ Going deeper"
    - Regulators are paying attention. In September 2025, the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) opened an inquiry into seven companies whose chatbots act like companions. One thing it asked was how they use or share personal information from users' chats.
    - On the training side, researchers have shown that a model can sometimes repeat text from its training data word for word. A 2020 study by Nicholas Carlini and others pulled hundreds of exact pieces of text out of GPT-2, including some people's names and contact details. This is why "Does the service train on my chats?" is an important question. Policies differ from service to service, so it's hard to say one thing about all of them.

## Try it yourself

Pick one message you sent, or plan to send, to a chatbot. Check it like this:

1. Use the table above. Does it contain any of those kinds of details?
2. If it does, blank them out (change names, delete numbers) and rewrite it.
3. Does the blanked version still get you the answer you want? Most of the time it does.
4. Open your app's settings and find the option about how your chats are used.

## Use it today

1. **Pause before you hit send.** Ask yourself: "Would I be okay writing this on a postcard?"
2. **Ask with blanked text, then fill in the real details yourself.** For example, write "Person A," and after you get the draft, swap in the real name.
3. **For work, check your workplace's rules first.** If your workplace has an approved AI tool, use that one.
4. **Be extra careful with other people's details.** You decide about your own information. Other people's information may need their permission.
5. **Never paste passwords or keys.** When you paste code, make sure no keys are hiding in it.

## Common mix-ups

- "Only I can see my chats." How the company stores and uses them differs by service. Check the settings and the privacy policy.
- "If I delete it, it's gone for good." Deletion rules differ by service. There may be a storage period or exceptions.
- "If I blank things out, I can't really ask." Even with details hidden, you can get most of the help you need.
