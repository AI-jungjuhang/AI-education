# What changes between free and paid?

<p class="ep-meta"><span>Ch. 4 · 4.1</span><span>About 5 min read</span><span>2026-11-09</span></p>

!!! abstract "What you'll learn on this page"
    - Free and paid plans usually differ in ==how much you can use, which models you get, and which features==. (Data settings differ by service and plan, so check those separately.)
    - Decide by asking whether your work actually needs what paid adds.
    - Prices and features change often, so check the service's own pages each time you decide.

!!! warning "Please note"
    We don't list prices or plan names on this page. Plans change so often that they'd be out of date the moment we printed them. Instead, here are ways to compare that won't go stale.

## An amusement park pass

At an amusement park, a basic ticket gets you on a few rides. A day pass lets you skip some lines and ride more. Some rides still cost extra, even with the pass.

Chatbots are similar. The free version is plenty for a lot of people. If you use it a lot, or for harder work, paying can help. But paid isn't always more accurate. Hallucinations and limits are still there when you pay.

!!! tip "In 10-year-old words"
    The difference between free and paid is how much you can do, and how big a job you can do. First ask whether your work needs that.

## Five things to compare

| Compare | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| How much you can use | How many messages per day or per few hours? What happens when I hit the limit? |
| Models | Can I use bigger, smarter models? How fast are they? |
| Features | Can it search the web, read uploaded files, make images, talk by voice, handle long documents? |
| Data settings | Can I stop my chats from being used for training? With a work account, can admins see my chats? |
| Reliability | Does it slow down or stop when lots of people are using it? Is there customer support? |

## When paid makes sense, and when it doesn't

**Times to think about paying**

- You use it a lot every day and keep hitting the free limit.
- You need more than the free limit allows, like often uploading long documents to analyze.
- Work depends on it, so speed and reliability matter.
- You use it for work and need data protection settings. (A personal paid plan does not by itself protect company data. Check your workplace's approval and business plans first.)

**Times free is enough**

- You ask questions now and then, or polish your writing.
- You're still learning how to use it. (How you ask often makes a bigger difference.)
- You'll check the results yourself anyway.

## How to decide

1. **Use the free version for a week first.** Write down which jobs hit the limit.
2. **Look at what got blocked.** Were the jobs you couldn't finish actually important?
3. **Try paid for one month at a time.** For a subscription, find out how to cancel before you start.
4. **Check that it helps.** Give the same job to the free and paid versions, and compare.

!!! note "★ Going deeper"
    - Anthropic's guide to choosing a model says to balance **capabilities, speed, and cost**, and then to test your choice on your real work. It's written for developers, but the same idea works for everyone. The biggest model isn't always best. For simple jobs, a smaller, faster model can fit better.
    - Some services let you pick the model yourself. You can use a small model for easy questions and a big one for hard reasoning.
    - Developers who use a model through an API usually pay by the token. That's where the tokens from Chapter 1 turn into money.

## Try it yourself

Keep a use log for one week.

| Date | What you asked for | Hit the limit? | Happy with the result? |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |

After a week, count the days when you hit the limit and the job mattered. That number is your reason to think about paying.

## Use it today

1. **Decide about paying by looking at your log.** If you decide by feel, you end up with subscriptions you don't use.
2. **Compare with the same question.** Ask the free and paid versions (or two different services) the exact same thing and see the difference.
3. **For work, check your workplace's policy first.** Using a personal plan with company material may break the rules.
4. **Read the data section of the terms.** Different plans can have different data rules.
5. **Check the official pricing page yourself.** Prices in blog posts and videos may be out of date.

## Common mix-ups

- "Paid means no hallucinations." Paid versions get things wrong too. You still need the checking habit.
- "The expensive model is better at everything." For some jobs, a small, fast model is the better fit.
- "Free is useless." Free is enough for many jobs. Learning how to ask comes first.
